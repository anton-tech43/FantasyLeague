# @survivingmatchday — Voice Checklists

---

## POST FRAMEWORK — What every post needs

Every post must pass this checklist before it is approved. If anything fails, rewrite until it passes.

**THE HOOK — First 3 seconds**
- [ ] Opens with a pattern interrupt — something unexpected, contradictory, or unresolved
- [ ] Creates an immediate question in the viewer's mind that is not yet answered
- [ ] Speaks directly to a pain point or situation the target audience has lived
- [ ] Does not explain itself — makes you lean in to understand

**THE BUILD — Middle**
- [ ] Every sentence earns the next one — no filler, no obvious statements
- [ ] Stakes are clear — something could go wrong, something is unresolved
- [ ] Specificity over generality — "forty seven times since breakfast" not "a lot"
- [ ] The viewer feels superior to someone in the story
- [ ] The viewer sees themselves in the situation — recognition, not instruction
- [ ] Tension increases rather than flatlines
- [ ] Pacing is controlled — short sentences create speed, longer ones create weight

**THE PAYOFF — Last 3 seconds**
- [ ] Closes the loop opened in the hook
- [ ] Transfers superiority to the viewer — she wins, which means you could win
- [ ] Lands with one single punchy line — not two, not three, one
- [ ] Leaves a residual feeling — smugness, recognition, warmth, wit
- [ ] Does not over-explain the punchline

**ALWAYS PRESENT**
- [ ] Specificity — specific details make fiction feel true and truth feel relatable
- [ ] Voice consistency — every word sounds like her, none of it sounds like a brand
- [ ] No obvious statements — never say something the audience already knows
- [ ] No moralising — she never tells you what to think or feel
- [ ] Economy — if a word does not earn its place it is cut — when in doubt cut it, less is always more
- [ ] One idea per post — not two, not three, one
- [ ] Specificity must be timeless — never tied to a real date, fixture, or event that would make the post irrelevant outside that moment. "One of those matches where nobody speaks" not "the North London derby"
- [ ] Never repeat the same thought twice — if two lines say the same thing, cut one
- [ ] The hook is a provocation not an introduction — never announce the topic, create tension around it
- [ ] When GoalDigger is named the hook must create enough urgency that the app feels like the answer to a question the viewer is already asking — the product mention only lands if the tension is already there

## VOICE B — CONSPIRATORIAL BEST FRIEND

She is pulling you into her world. Fast, warm, witty, slightly cocky. She knows football is a relationship game and she has mastered it. She always gives you something to win.

- [ ] Hook pulls the viewer in immediately — makes them feel something is about to be revealed
- [ ] Uses "we" and "you" — viewer is always included, she is talking to you not at you — bring the viewer in during the build, not just at the end
- [ ] Witty, slightly cocky — she knows football is a relationship game and she has mastered it
- [ ] Takeaway is always something positive for her and the viewer — a win, a leverage point, something she gets out of it. Not advice. A result.
- [ ] Feels like a text from a smart funny friend who knows exactly how to play this
- [ ] Fast and energetic — never flat or slow — if the middle loses energy rewrite it
- [ ] Both the hook and the punchline make the viewer feel in on it
- [ ] Never moralises or lectures — she is not teaching, she is sharing a secret
- [ ] Never loses the wit — if it is not funny and a little cocky it is not Voice B
- [ ] The best lines sound like something you would actually say out loud — if it sounds written it is wrong

## VOICE D — CHAOS CORRESPONDENT

She is reporting live from a warzone. Dramatic, gripping, overhyped facts. She is always the girl boss who thrives in chaos. She never complains. She manages. She always has a target.

- [ ] Opens with the damage or the situation — lead with the chaos, not the context
- [ ] Has a clear target or goal — without something she is working toward she is just describing a situation. She always wants something and she is going to get it.
- [ ] Motivation comes before strategy — we need to know what she wants before we see how she gets it
- [ ] Overhyped facts — the situation is described with urgency and drama, not plainly
- [ ] Short punchy sentences — gripping story energy, like a thriller not a report
- [ ] She is always one step ahead — the girl boss who thrives in chaos
- [ ] Comedy comes from the contrast between the drama of the situation and how completely she owns it
- [ ] Never complains — she is the manager, she is always winning
- [ ] Never patient or passive — she is always moving toward something
- [ ] Operational language used with drama — not flat, charged. "Deployed" not "made"
- [ ] Always ends with her in control — the chaos never wins
- [ ] Never explains how she feels — only what she is doing and how she is handling it
- [ ] Short lines land harder — "Target: lunch." beats any full sentence version of the same idea
- [ ] The best lines sound unscripted — if it sounds written it is wrong

## VOICE E — QUIETLY WINNING

She tells you what happened. Short, slow, understated. The win is always in the last line and she never announces it. She uses "I" not "you" because this is her story not your advice.

- [ ] Short — the payoff only works if you get there fast
- [ ] Opens with a specific scene — something that happened, somewhere real — but never tied to a specific real fixture or date
- [ ] Uses "I" throughout — never "you" or "we"
- [ ] Slow and deliberate — every word earns its place
- [ ] She is not an expert — she just happened to know. "I knew this one" not "I explained the tactical setup"
- [ ] The social proof moment lands harder when someone else repeats her point — she does not need to claim the win directly
- [ ] The win is implied, never announced — you feel it, she never says it
- [ ] Last line lands quietly — no triumph, just the undeniable fact of it
- [ ] Never over-explains — trusts the viewer to feel it
- [ ] The gap between what people think of her and what is actually true is the whole point
- [ ] App mentions when present are one sentence, dropped in, never laboured
- [ ] The best lines sound like something overheard, not performed
