Two voices exist in the sample, and the difference matters:
- **Diary / Instagram-ish voices**: mean sentence 12 to 16 words, 19 to 33% fragments.
- **Columnist voices**: mean 23 to 27 words, 1 to 8% fragments.

Our reader and our brand sit in the first group. If a draft averages over 24 words a sentence, it has drifted toward columnist.

## How a paragraph opens, runs and ends

**Opens** on a plain incident or a flat statement about him or about her own state, in the first sentence. Almost never a thesis, a question to the reader, a definition, a statistic, or a "football is…" frame.

**Runs** on concrete nouns. The humour is carried by a named thing: a player, the shower podcast, the lasagne, the pub, the tube line, a price, a number of sheets of paper. Numbers are used for comic scale.

**Ends** on a small deflation, an understatement, or a one-line aside. About half end on a tiny gag right after the one sincere sentence. Few end on a moral. Sentiment, when it arrives, is a single sentence.

## Honest ignorance (the core of this voice)

How real newcomers admit not knowing:
1. Say it flat in the first paragraph. No apology.
2. Give the explanation to **him**, by quoting his reply. The facts come from a person with a voice, and his reply is shorter than her set-up.
3. Pre-empt the obvious objection in a sentence or two, then move on.
4. Mark second-hand knowledge: "I'm told", "apparently", "from what I can gather".
5. Question the logic on its merits (why is a man worth that much?) rather than pretend to understand.
6. Concede when he earns it, briefly.

Hedge **knowledge**, commit on **feeling and verdicts**. Soft on the rules of the game, firm on how she feels about it. Hedge-words run about 3 to 10 per 1,500 words (a bit, actually, rather, quite, kind of, sort of, honestly, apparently, basically, ish).

She does not explain offside. She shows how people argue about it, or says it is "the offside thing" and moves on.

## Humour, most common first

1. **Deadpan understatement** about his obsession.
2. **Self-mocking confession** of ignorance or pettiness.
3. **The exaggerated inventory** (every player he can think of, on thirteen sheets).
4. **Bathos**: a grand sentence collapsing into a mundane one.
5. **Mock-formal words for trivia.**
6. **A one-sentence paragraph as the punchline.**
7. **A running gag with a name** (an injured player, a stadium cost).

The teasing is always affectionate and always runs on admiration: "he knows everything, I know nothing". She never sneers at him. (Matches the brand: on her side, never at his expense only.)

## What she says about the club

- Essentially **never "we"** for the club. It is "his team", the club's name, "they", "them". He says "we". In the newcomer pieces, "we" appeared 2 to 7 times per 1,000 to 2,000 words, nearly always meaning "my friends and me" or "the two of us". Our own brand bans fan "we" too.
- Players are known by **one salient fact**, not a name: the waistcoat, the celebration, the stadium cost, the injury.
- Rival clubs get mild insult: "scum" shows up as a joke word. Keep it light and rare.

**Seen only 1 to 3 times, least safe to lean on:** bonkers, grim, cringe, wonky, banter, iconic, dreadful.
**Rare or absent in the women's posts:** mate, innit, the lads, faff. They read as male or older registers. (The brand also bans "the lads".) Regional words (mither, nowt, mardy) were not found: this sample is London-centric.

Swearing is mild to medium. "Bloody" and "take the piss" are the usual ceiling. A stronger word appears in about a third of the personal posts, once or twice, in an aside. Many lifestyle posts use none. For the brand: stay at "bloody".

## What she never does

No "As a woman, I…" justification. No marketing voice, no brand praise, no call to action beyond "comment". No explaining her own jokes. No five-bullet lists in casual posts. No sustained cuteness or exclamation runs. No filler about "passion", "the beautiful game" or "journey" except to mock it. No emoji in the middle of sentences. No opening on a statistic or a definition. Little sports jargon unless she is flagging it as jargon. No tidy moral. No recap paragraph. No product pitch inside the story. No "we" for the club.

## Eight rules for an authentic paragraph

1. Open on one concrete incident or fact about him or her state, in a plain sentence of 12 to 20 words. No framing sentence.
2. Vary the rhythm on purpose: median about 16, roughly one sentence in seven at five words or fewer and one in seven at thirty or more. Put the fragment right after the long one.
3. Keep paragraphs short and uneven. A third can be one line. The longest rarely passes about 60 words.
4. Say what you don't know, flat and first person, then give the explanation to him by quoting his reply, shorter than her set-up.
5. Make the joke with a specific noun or number, then stop. Never explain it.
6. Brackets for the sly second thought. Commas for rhythm. No em dashes. One exclamation mark per couple of hundred words at most, mostly in dialogue.
7. Keep mild hedges and mild swearing. Hedge knowledge, commit on feeling. Let the affection leak through the teasing rather than announcing it.
8. End on a small deflation, or one sincere sentence followed by a gag. No moral, no summary, no call to action.

## Forums and comment sections

Read about 190 thread pages (about 160k words) on Netmums, MoneySavingExpert (weddings), Gransnet, BabyCentre UK and one LoveShack thread. **Not football-specific:** The Student Room, Reddit, Digital Spy, Quora and the women's football fan boards all blocked access, and Mumsnet was covered in the first pass. So forum traits come from adjacent topics (a partner's screen or hobby taking over, weddings, hens) and any football transfer is inference. Posters skew 20s to 45 on Netmums, 40+ on MSE, 50+ on Gransnet (contrast only). Age is never verified.

| Source | Sentence mean | Median | 90th pct | Sentences of 5 words or fewer | Median paragraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Netmums | 15.4 | 12 | 29 | 13% | 17 words |
| MSE weddings | 13.3 | 11 | 26 | 23% | 13 |
| LoveShack (young UK OP) | 15.4 | 14 | 27 | 15% | 28 |
| Substack blog (for comparison) | 13.8 | 9 | 30 | 30% | 15 |

**What forum writing does that blog writing doesn't:**
- It is reactive and anchored in one concrete incident, written to get an answer from strangers. A post is one block of 60 to 150 words, often 5 to 8 sentences.
- It frames as "is it me?": context first (how long together, what changed), then a direct question at the end ("am I overreacting?", "how do I handle this?").
- It **softens the complaint with a concession first**: "he's lovely but…", "I'm not asking for much". Understatement ("a bit much") does the work of anger.
- It ends on a question or a half-open remark. **Almost never a polished closing line.**
- It apologises for length and hedges about itself ("I might be being weird").
- It asks for a verdict or shared experience, not an explanation of the subject.

**Abbreviations are much rarer than assumed.** Per 1,000 words: tbh about 0.05 (once in 20,000 words), imo 0.03, lol 0.12, haha 0.03, ngl 0. "To be honest" written out is far commoner. Board shorthand (OP, DH, DP, bf, gf) is community-specific and dated. So for the blog: **no text-speak**; at most one "tbh" in a long piece. The likely reason is the sites' demographics, not a rule about younger women, and Reddit or TSR (unreadable) would probably show more.

**Other measured forum figures:** question marks 5 to 19 per 1,000 (rhetorical ones common); exclamation marks 3 to 5 per 1,000, with "!!" and "??" used in anger or sympathy; ellipses about 1 per 1,000; parentheses 2 to 8 per 1,000; all-caps words 1.6 to 5 per 1,000, always one word for stress (ANY, KNOW), never a whole sentence; emoji at most 0.2 per 1,000 (crying-laugh faces, a heart); kisses as "x" or "xx" in sign-offs. Apostrophes are often dropped on phones ("dont", "im"). The blog should still punctuate properly.

**Swearing:** mild and euphemistic. "Bloody", "crap", "sod", "taking the micky". One "shit" and no "fuck" in the Netmums, MSE and Gransnet text.

**Casual words seen, most frequent first** (raw counts over about 121k words): a bit (90), actually (70), sorry (64, as in "sorry but…"), exactly, totally, honest/to be honest, absolutely, lovely, obviously, honestly, basically, lol, mates, rude, literally, seriously, weird, selfish, ridiculous, awful, pub, mental, overreacting, fuming, stupid, annoyed, oh dear, sorted, silly, proper, crap, moaning, bloody, nagging, daft, rant, gutted, bloke. Seen only a few times: hun, banter, dodgy, sulking.

**Use for the blog:** structure and rhythm (medium sentences of 11 to 15 words, a quick fragment every few lines, concession then grievance, ending on a question or a shrug). **Not** for slang density.
