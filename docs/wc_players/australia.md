# Australia — World Cup 2026

Australia (the Socceroos) head to the 2026 FIFA World Cup under head coach Tony Popovic, who took charge in September 2024 after replacing Graham Arnold. Goalkeeper Mathew Ryan captains the side at his fourth World Cup, equalling the Australian record held by Tim Cahill and Mark Milligan. Popovic named his 26-man squad on 1 June 2026. Australia are drawn in Group D alongside co-hosts the United States, Paraguay and Turkey, opening their campaign against Turkey in Vancouver before meeting the USA and Paraguay.

## Key players

**Mathew Ryan** (Goalkeeper, Levante) — Captain at his fourth World Cup, equalling the Australian record. He made a crucial penalty save in his 100th cap to help secure a 2–1 win over Saudi Arabia and qualification, and joined Levante on a free transfer in August 2025, helping them avoid relegation with five clean sheets in their final eight games.

**Harry Souttar** (Defender, Leicester) — Australia's most physically imposing defender and a genuine aerial threat from set pieces. He is set to appear at his second World Cup.

**Jordan Bos** (Defender, Feyenoord) — Left-back who moved to Feyenoord on a four-year deal in July 2025 and became the first Australian named Eredivisie Player of the Month (September 2025). His form in Rotterdam puts him in contention to start ahead of Aziz Behich.

**Alessandro Circati** (Defender, Parma) — Born in Italy and raised in Perth, a former Italy youth international who chose Australia. He recovered from an ACL rupture suffered in September 2024 to feature in qualification and has captained the national team. He made 32 league starts for Parma in their 13th-place Serie A finish.

**Jackson Irvine** (Midfielder, FC St. Pauli) — One of Australia's most important central midfielders, combining defensive discipline with technical quality. He is on track to feature at his third World Cup.

**Nestory Irankunda** (Attacker, Watford) — One to watch: one of Australia's most exciting young talents. After a difficult spell at Bayern Munich following a record A-League transfer, he joined Championship side Watford, scoring four goals in his debut season there. The Guardian named him among the 60 best young talents in world football in 2023, the only Australian listed.
