# Mexico — World Cup 2026

Mexico are co-hosts of the 2026 World Cup and qualified automatically, drawn as the top seed in Group A alongside South Africa, South Korea and the Czech Republic (Czechia). The squad is managed by Javier Aguirre, in his third stint in charge of El Tri after previously leading the team at the 2002 and 2010 World Cups. Edson Álvarez (Fenerbahçe) is the captain. Mexico arrive in form, having won the CONCACAF Nations League in March 2025 and the Gold Cup in July 2025; pre-tournament simulations make the hosts favourites to progress from Group A. The wider aim is to break a run of seven consecutive tournaments in which Mexico has not advanced past the round of 16.

## Key players

**Edson Álvarez** (Defender, Fenerbahçe) — Captain and on-field leader of the side, with around 97 caps for Mexico ahead of the tournament.
**Raúl Jiménez** (Attacker, Fulham) — At 34, the team's main attacking reference, arriving at a home World Cup after reaching double figures for goals in a strong Premier League season with Fulham.
**Santiago Giménez** (Attacker, AC Milan) — Leads the attack alongside Jiménez, though he came through an injury-affected season at Milan, so his starting role is not guaranteed.
**Álvaro Fidalgo** (Midfielder, Club América) — Naturalised midfielder named among the squad's central-midfield options.
**Orbelín Pineda** (Midfielder, AEK Athens) — Experienced midfielder listed among Aguirre's key central options.
**Guillermo Ochoa** (Goalkeeper, AEL) — At 40, named to a record sixth World Cup squad, joining Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo as the only players to appear at six different World Cups, in the final tournament of his career.

## One to watch

**Gilberto Mora** (Midfielder, Club Tijuana) — A 17-year-old attacking midfielder who could become the youngest Mexican man to feature at a World Cup. He has drawn reported European interest (including Real Madrid) and already set youth records, including becoming the youngest player to win a senior international title at 16 years and 265 days. His directness gives Aguirre an unpredictable attacking option.
