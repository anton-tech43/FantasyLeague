# M1: 31, marketing manager, Enfield. Husband is Spurs. Mode: "Came to impress"

## 1. FIRST LOOK

It's an app that keeps an eye on his team for me and tells me, in normal English, what happened and what to say about it.

**Would I download it? Maybe, leaning yes.** "Keep up. Or wind him up." is exactly me, and the banner sold it. Three things stopped it being a clear yes:
- **The description is stuck in the summer.** It says "This summer's tournament is about to make a month of this", "kicks off June 11" and "updates on his country". It's late October. I work in marketing, and a store page that's four months out of date makes me think the app has been abandoned.
- **0 ratings.**
- **"No subscription, no ads, no tracking."** But the privacy box right underneath says "Data Used to Track You: Identifiers; Usage Data". Which is it? I'd still probably download it because it's free.

## 2. THE FIRST SESSION (pretending Liverpool = Spurs)

- **00 Welcome:** carry on. "You're here. He has no idea." made me smile. It feels like a secret, which is the fun bit.

- **02 Their name:** carry on. "They're my… Partner" is fine. The heading "their name" was slightly odd when the whole thing is pitched at boyfriends, but I didn't care.

  - Carry on. I picked "Came to impress".

- **08 Notifications:** carry on. I'd say yes, because a heads-up before kickoff is the whole reason I'm here. "It only works if we can reach you" is a bit needy, but honest.
- **09 Calendar:**
  - Carry on, but I'd tap "Not now". 25 football matches in my calendar is not happening. My work calendar is busy enough.
  - (The store page said 15, by the way.)
- **12 First screen (feed):** carry on, but frowning.
  - A third of the screen is a "Notifications are off" box.
  - The big headline is "liverpool. bournemouth in the. quarters.", which reads like a typo.

  - The analogy ("Acing the tricky first date and finding out the next one is with someone much more your type") is nice, but it needed the plain fact first.
  - The "9+" badge next to the club name: no idea what it is.

**Was that worth it? Not yet.**

**What did I actually get?** One player's stats, the manager's name and the fact that they won their last game. And one line to say that I don't fully understand.

**Which setup step didn't I understand the point of?**
- "How far do you want to take this?": nothing tells me what's different between the three.
- Also "Here's how it works", which is a page about the app instead of the app.

**When did I first think "oh, that's useful"?**
- A flicker at **05 Meet the boss** ("loves or wants fired this week").
- The real one came after the setup, on **14 Matchday**: "Liverpool beat Chelsea 6-5 on penalties after a 1-1 draw… Through to the quarter-finals." Finally something plain and clear.

### 13–17

- **What I'd tap first:** the big top card (13), because I wanted to know what "drew Bournemouth" meant.
- **13 Article: partly delivered.** The headline explains it: they beat Chelsea on penalties and *then* got Bournemouth in the quarter-final draw. "League Cup (Carabao Cup)" is underlined, which is good. But the "Things to say" just repeats "drew Bournemouth… a good draw" without saying "draw = who you got picked to play". The "To impress" bar ("one more, for when he's listening") is intriguing, but it's collapsed, so I didn't know what was in it.
- **14 Matchday: delivered, then undid itself.**
  - The huge headline says **"Liverpool 1-1 Chelsea"** when they *won*. You have to read the small serif text to find out.
  - Then "Say this now: They are through. Ask him who he wants to draw in the quarter-finals". But the feed just told me they've already drawn Bournemouth. If I asked that, he'd look at me like I'd been asleep. **The app contradicts itself on screen two.**
- **15 Club page:** the same stale "Ask him who he wants to draw in th…" line again, at the very top. The basics ("The Reds, Last season: 5th, 60 points") are useful. The manager's face is there now.
- **16 My Turn:** "The Basics, 30 questions". It looks like homework. I'd only go in on a slow evening.
- **17 Tracking prompt:**
  - "Ask App Not to Track", obviously.
  - The store page literally said "no tracking". It popped up a second after I landed, before the app had shown me anything good.
  - Small trust dent.

## 6. THE TEST

At the pub, I'd say:

> "Maddison hadn't started a Premier League game since May 2025. Eighteen months out, first start back, he scored."

**Smart or exposed?** Mostly smart. It's a specific fact with a story in it, and his mates would nod. Slight risk someone says "well he'd come off the bench before", but the line says *started*, so I'm covered. (Close second, to wind him up: "The stadium is incredible. The trophy cabinet is… a work in progress.")

**Would I keep the app? Maybe.**
- **Why I'd keep it:** the lines that land (Maddison, Kane, the trophy cabinet, "He has gone quiet. Let him.") are exactly what I came for. And Say This, with SAFE vs BOLD, is the first thing that gives me an opinion of *my own* instead of "Ask him what he thinks."
- **Why I might not:**
  - Too much of the app is "ask him / he'll have a view", which is still me reacting to him.
  - It missed the Chelsea derby completely.
  - It woke me at 6am about Inter and Bodo/Glimt.
  - It contradicted itself on my second screen ("drew Bournemouth" vs "ask who he wants to draw").
- If the derby silence happened twice, I'd delete it.
