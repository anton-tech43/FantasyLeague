# M2: 26, recruitment, Manchester. Boyfriend Tom is a Liverpool fan, I'm meeting his mates at the pub. Mode: "Came to impress"

## 1. FIRST LOOK

It's an app that tells you what's going on with your boyfriend's team in normal English and gives you something to say back. **Maybe, leaning yes**, because it's free and I'm panicking about this weekend. "Keep up. Or wind him up." is honestly me.

What put me off on the store page:
- The description goes on about "This summer's tournament", "England" and "kicks off June 11". It's October. It reads like nobody has touched the page since summer, and his team isn't England.
- It says "No subscription, no ads, no tracking", but further down it says "Data Used to Track You: Identifiers; Usage Data". Which one is true? I work in recruitment and I read small print. That's a red flag.
- The screenshots are all Arsenal. Fine, but I'm Liverpool.
- One screenshot says "his next 15 matches". The app later says 25. Small, but it adds to the feeling that nobody's checking.
- 0 ratings. "Football for partners" is a decent subtitle.

The first image (the welcome screen) is fine: "You're here. He has no idea. Let's get you ready." I liked the secret-mission feel.

## 6. THE TEST

What I'd actually say, from Say This → His team just scored:

> "That's been coming."

I'd feel **smart and safe**. It sounds like I've been watching. Nobody asks a follow-up, and it doesn't sound swotted. If I'm feeling brave, my second would be the Bournemouth "five-win run" one, but only if the cup comes up.

**Would I keep the app?** Maybe. Leaning yes until Sunday, because the Matchday "Get to know Arsenal" and Say This bits are exactly what I need. But it said "Chelsea tomorrow" the day after Chelsea, told me to ask who he wants to draw after the draw had happened, and calls Gakpo both a midfielder and an attacker. If it ever makes me say something wrong in front of his mates, it's deleted that night. Being caught out is the one thing I came here to avoid.

## 7. BEST AND WORST

- **Best moment**: Say This, "That's been coming." [SAFE] with "If they'd had chances before it went in. Sounds like you were watching properly." It's usable, honest about the risk, and tells me exactly when to say it.
