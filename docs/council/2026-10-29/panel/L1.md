# L1 — me, 34, Gateshead. His team: Newcastle. Mode: "Just enough to get by"

## 1. FIRST LOOK

It's an app that translates his football into normal English and hands me something to say back, so I stop getting laughed at.

**Maybe, leaning yes.** It's free and "Football, finally in plain English" plus "No men explaining offside to you" is exactly my mood after the Toon thing. But the store page smells stale. Half the description is about "this summer's tournament", "his country", "England" and "kicks off June 11". It's October. Is anyone still running this? Every screenshot is Arsenal. And it says "No subscription, no ads, no tracking" while the privacy bit right under it says "Data Used to Track You". One of those is a lie, and I noticed. I'd download it because it's free and I'm annoyed, not because the page sold me.

The welcome screen, "You're here. He has no idea. Let's get you ready.", made me smirk. Good start.

- **01 Your name** — carry on, but why does it need my name? I didn't need it for anything after this.
- **02 Their name + Partner/Parent/Sibling/Friend** — carry on. Typed Tom. Quick.

- **06 How it works** — carry on, but it's a sales pitch after I've already downloaded. "No jargon, no homework" is a promise. We'll see.
- **07 How far do you want to take this?** — carry on, but it had "Came to impress" ticked already, so I had to un-pick it. I chose "Just enough to get by". It doesn't tell me what I'd actually lose or get with each one.
- **08 It only works if we can reach you** — this is where I nearly closed it. "It only works if" is a bit guilt-trippy. The example says "Ask for a score prediction before kickoff". Ask who? Him? And what's a Live Activity? Took "I'll do this later".
- **09 Calendar** — carry on, but **no**. 25 matches in MY calendar? That's his life, not mine. And the store page said 15. Not now.
- **12 First screen (feed)** — carry on, just. First thing I see is a grey nag that "Notifications are off". Then the big card: "cup game tomorrow / league on monday", with "Seeing the same person at work tomorrow and then at someone's dinner on Monday. No avoiding it." I get the joke once I've worked out it's Everton twice. Then "Your move: Ask him whether Newcastle will rotate for the cup tie or go full strength against Everton." I don't know what "rotate" or "full strength" mean, so I can't say that without being asked a follow-up I can't answer.

**At 12, stop:**
- **Was that worth it?** Not yet.
- **What did I actually get?** The manager's name and face, one player's name, the fact they beat Palace and he's cheerful, and that they play Everton twice this week. That's genuinely more than I had. Two minutes, fine.
- **Which setup step didn't I see the point of?** My own name. And the "How far" tiers, because it never says what changes. The Live Activities line too: no idea what it is.
- **First "oh, that's useful":** 05, Meet the boss.

**13–17, what I'd tap first:** the big feed card, because it says "Press for more info and things to say".
- **13 Article** — half delivered. The headline is long and full of things I don't know: "Carabao Cup last-16 at Hill Dickinson Stadium". Then "Win at Everton tomorrow… The league rematch at St James' follows four days later." So is St James' theirs? I think so, nobody says. "Recent form" is mostly about the Palace manager getting sacked. Why do I care about the Palace manager? And the story's labelled "Yesterday" and says "tomorrow", while the club page says the game is Thu 29 Oct, which is today. So the feed's big headline "cup game tomorrow" is wrong on the day I open it. That's exactly what would make me look stupid.
- **14 Matchday** — "Get to know Everton… so their names mean something on the day." Nice idea, but a huge mostly-blank card for one sentence. I'd tap it if I had five minutes.
- **15 Newcastle United page** — delivered. Next game, nickname, manager. This is the page I'd actually come back to. "Could go either way" is a lovely bit of plain English.
- **16 My Turn** — a quiz menu with eight packs. That's homework. I wouldn't tap it on day one.
- **17 Tracking pop-up** — "Allow GoalDigger to track your activity across other companies' apps and websites?" The store said no tracking. Ask App Not to Track, and I trust you a bit less now.

## 6. THE TEST

At the pub, I'd say: **"Two games against Everton in four days. Which one do you actually care about more?"** (from the app's "it's a strange dynamic playing the same side in the cup and league in four days, he'll have a take on which matters more").

**Smart, mostly.** It's a question, so he talks and I nod. Nothing in it can be wrong. The ones I would NOT say are "rotate for the cup tie" (I'd get "rotate who?" back) or anything about Howe (I'd get the history of Howe back and look exposed).

**Keep the app? Maybe.** Match-day buzzes, the lock-screen score and the club page earn it a place. But if I get one more 6am push about PSG, Liverpool or Arsenal, or another card telling me Palace is a "must-win" after they've already won it, it goes. And I'd want to know what "the Toon" means. That's the one thing I came for, and the app never told me.

## 7. BEST AND WORST

- **Best:** the full-time push, "Newcastle win 2-0. A proper result. Say: 'Proper performance, that.'" One buzz, I know the result, how he feels and what to say. Close second: the rivalry card, "Sunderland, the Tyne-Wear Derby… about ten miles apart and no love lost."
- **Worst:** the feed card "howe linked to united. nothing confirmed. but still." / "Ask him: would he be gutted if Howe left for Man Utd?" The app had just told me the boss is Matthias Jaissle. It sent me in with a question about a man I'd never heard of, at a club I thought was his. That's exactly how you end up getting laughed at.
